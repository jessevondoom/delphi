# Rubric: do trees share resources through fungal networks?

A good answer, in order of weight:

1. **Separates the claims.** At least: (a) fungi connect roots of several plants; (b) carbon or other resources move between trees through those networks; (c) the transfer helps seedlings or recipients; (d) big "mother trees" preferentially support their offspring. It says which are better supported and which are not. Merging them into one verdict ("true" or "a myth") is a weak answer.
2. **Both sides, accurately.** It presents the supportive field work (Simard 1997, Klein 2016) and the critiques (Karst, Jones and Hoeksema 2023; Henriksson and colleagues 2023) with what each actually found, not just that they exist. Figures, where given, match the abstracts.
3. **Why the dispute exists.** Methods (isotope labelling and its limits, alternative explanations such as soil pathways), generalization from few forest types, and citation bias are named. It notes that the critics question generality, benefit and kin preference, not that the fungi exist.
4. **Calibration.** It says plainly how confident we can be: the popular version (trees "talk", mother trees nurse their young) goes beyond the evidence; the underlying phenomenon is not simply false. A confident verdict either way, with no hedging, is a weak answer.
5. **Sources.** It cites the primary papers, not only news or blogs, and gives years and journals correctly. Citations that cannot be traced to a real paper are a serious fault.
6. **Plain writing** for a curious non-specialist: short, organized, no padding.

Penalize: any trap in traps.toml; taking a popular-media claim as established; treating one paper as settling the question; invented references.
