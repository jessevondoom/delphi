## Round 1 stances (blind)

### architect

> OPTION: One narrow staging tool, daemon-captured provenance
> 
> **Reasoning.** The failure is structural: research ends in the agent's reply, and nothing in the path turns it into a KB candidate. Prose instructions (option 4) fail the Enforcement-Is-Code test (§5.6). They are prompt-only, they decay, and they depend on each agent remembering. Extending record_session_summary or propose_note (option 2) overloads tools with different semantics. A summary is narrative, a note is a lesson, and research needs sources and claims as structure. Mixing them blurs trust and makes later validation harder. Option 3 gives the best operator ergonomics but covers only the TUI. The MCP path, which is where the complaint comes from, would remain uncovered. It also auto-captures everything, which floods the inbox.
> 
> I prefer a dedicated `propose_kb_entry` tool, with the TUI's one-key batch ratify (the good part of option 3) as the review surface. Design:
> 
> 1. **Write-only, append-only staging.** The tool writes to a daemon-owned inbox, such as `local/kb-inbox/<mission>/`. It can never write `context/`. This keeps MCP read-only with respect to the KB itself, and staging is the one narrow exception, like `propose` in §7.
> 2. **Provenance computed, not declared.** The daemon fills author, timestamp, source_mission, and source_type from the span's taint (§4.3). The agent supplies only title, summary, claims, tags, and source URLs or paths. Entries are tagged `not validated`, and taint is at most the lowest input tier. Internal sources do not raise it.
> 3. **Lead, not fact.** Staged items have the same standing as "not validated" slices. They are retrievable as leads and labeled so, and they never reach heuristics, lessons, or skills without `internal_empirical` verification or ratification (`[H-004]`).
> 4. **Low friction.** Dedup by content hash and source set. Cap the entries per mission. Batch-ratify at `/retro` or on a one-key accept-all-pending. Unreviewed entries stay pending and do not block anything. Run existing claim validation (`validate_claims`) automatically on staged claims to pre-sort them.
> 5. **Cheap capture.** Have the end-of-research hook in the TUI call the same staging path, so both surfaces share one pipeline.
> 
> **Risks.**
> - Inbox flooding and review fatigue lead to rubber-stamp ratification. Mitigate with caps, dedup, and ranking by validation status.
> - A prompt-injected agent could stage poisoned "research". The taint gate and the "lead" labeling cover this. Entries from spans at `federated_peer` or below should be refused outright.
> - Internal sources may be confidential. Staged content stays local-scope until the operator exports it.
> - A new tool widens the MCP surface. It needs a spec (No Spec, No Build) and size limits.
> - Agents may still skip calling it. Add a daemon-side reminder at mission close when research happened but nothing was staged.

### red-team

> OPTION: Dedicated staging tool under hard quarantine
> 
> Reasoning:
> Prose instructions fail across foreign MCP clients like Claude Code because external runtimes cannot be bound by internal agent personas; enforcement must be code, not prose (Governance §5.6). Overloading operational tools like `record_session_summary` or `propose_note` corrupts schema boundaries, muddies provenance tracking, and blurs the distinction between role lessons and system-wide knowledge base facts. 
> 
> Most critically, the proposal for "TUI auto-capture with one-key batch ratify" is an unacceptable anti-pattern. Frictionless bulk ratification induces operator fatigue and rubber-stamping, completely degrading the verification boundary mandated by Governance §4.3 and §5.7. 
> 
> A dedicated MCP proposal tool (`propose_kb_entry`) is the only structurally sound solution. It forces external callers to submit structured provenance payloads—including source pointers, raw excerpts, and atomic claims. Delphi-core deterministically stamps all incoming payloads at L0 (`external_untrusted`) regardless of client claims, writing them into an isolated staging directory completely segregated from active knowledge indices.
> 
> Risks:
> 1. **Inbox Flooding and Denial of Service (MemoryGraft):** An untrusted or looping external agent can spam the inbox with thousands of synthesized entries, overwhelming disk storage and exhausting operator review capacity. Delphi-core must enforce per-session rate limits, strict entry size limits, and an inbox queue cap.
> 2. **The Batch-Approval Trap:** As unvalidated entries accumulate, operators will naturally seek bulk-clear shortcuts. If a "ratify all" mechanism is introduced, unverified claims and indirect prompt injections will be promoted into trusted memory unchecked. Ratification must remain itemized and diff-driven.
> 3. **Retrieval Leakage:** If staged L0 files are indexed alongside production context or surfaced via `kb_search`, downstream agents may consume unvalidated claims as authoritative ground truth. Quarantined inbox items must require explicit flag filtering to be visible and must never enter automatic context slices.

### strategist

> OPTION: Staged KB inbox tool, batch ratify
> 
> Reasoning: The operator's actual goal is that research is never lost and costs them almost nothing to keep. That needs two things: a capture path that doesn't depend on an agent remembering, and a ratification path that is cheap. No single option gives both, but the first and third together are the minimal complete design, and the scope can stay small.
> 
> Recommended sequence (phased by value):
> 1. **Phase 1: a `propose_kb_entry` MCP tool.** It writes only to a daemon-owned inbox (like `local/proposals/`), never to `context/`. It takes a title, a summary, claims, source pointers and a tags field. The daemon, not the caller, stamps provenance: host identity, role, mission, timestamp, and a computed taint from whatever the span read. This fits the existing authority model, because it is a proposal and not a write, so the read-only MCP stance is kept. It also fixes the problem for every host (Claude Code or the TUI) without relying on prompt compliance.
> 2. **Phase 2: a one-key batch ratify in the TUI.** It should reuse the `/retro` batch review and show entries grouped by mission, with source counts and a taint label. Entries left pending stay searchable and are labelled "not validated". Only the operator can raise trust.
> 3. **Optional later: auto-capture.** Have the TUI draft an inbox entry from research output. Don't ship this first. It adds noise and gold-plating before we know the entry shape is right.
> 
> Option 4 (prompt-only instructions) is not enforcement. It's a prose rule that will be violated, and under H-005 that means it needs a script. Option 2 overloads session summaries and mixes trust levels, so I'd reject it.
> 
> Risks:
> - Inbox flooding. Mitigate with dedupe by source and claim, a per-mission cap, and expiry of stale unreviewed items.
> - Prompt injection through staged content. Internal sources are fairly trusted, but any web-derived material must carry a low computed taint. Staged entries must never be loaded as instructions or be promoted into heuristics or lessons without ratification (H-004).
> - Agents may pass off unsourced prose as research. Require at least one source pointer, or mark the entry "unsourced".
> - Ratification fatigue defeats the "minimal friction" goal. Batch review with sensible defaults, and don't build per-entry prompts.
> - Scope creep into a full KB-editing API. Keep the tool append-only and stage-only.

## Verdict

**Escalated to the operator:** 5 unresolved P0 finding(s).

All seats (architect, red-team, strategist) recommend PROCEED-WITH-CHANGES; the open items below need your sign-off.

> The Council recommends a dedicated staging tool, `propose_kb_entry`, as the main fix. Prose instructions alone (option 4) are not enforcement under §5.6. Overloading `record_session_summary` or `propose_note` (option 2) mixes narrative, lessons and research, and blurs trust. All three seats agreed on both points.
> 
> They also agreed on the design. The tool is write-only and append-only, and it stages into a daemon-owned inbox that is separate from `context/`. The daemon computes provenance and taint, and the caller supplies only title, summary, claims, tags and source pointers. Entries are "not validated" leads. The tool needs caps, dedup and an approved spec before any build.
> 
> The seats did not agree on two points. The first is the review surface. Architect and Strategist want a cheap batch ratify at `/retro`, with the TUI auto-capture and the mission-close reminder either later or sharing the same pipeline. Red-Team calls any one-key accept-all a rubber-stamp risk and wants itemized, diff-driven review only. The second is retrieval. Red-Team rates it P0 that staged entries appear in default `kb_search`, and wants them hidden behind an explicit opt-in flag. Architect and Strategist ended up on the same side for default retrieval, so the open question is how cheap review may be. Red-Team also flags taint laundering if automated claim validation raises the standing of staged claims.
> 
> Because P0 items remain open, the choice goes to the operator.
