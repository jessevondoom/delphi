## 2. Open-Question Recommendation (Item 5: Widening Role Permissions via `delphi customize grant`)

**Recommendation: Keep `narrow-only` (`specs/001` §2.3) absolute for all built-in canonical roles. Do NOT allow `delphi customize grant` to widen built-in role permissions.**

- **Failure modes of allowing widening grants (`delphi customize grant`):**
  1. **Defeats structural separation of powers (`GOVERNANCE.md` §2, §5.1, `[REV-SEC-02]`):** Built-in role IDs (`scout`, `investigator`, `architect`, `strategist`, `red-team`) are hardcoded into downstream trust assumptions. If an operator widens `investigator` or `architect` to include `write: ["@worktree/**"]` or unrestricted shell execution via a personal waiver in `$DELPHI_HOME/operator/ratified.lock`, that grant silently applies across **every** workspace (`006-A1`), turning read-only discovery/audit nodes in `specs/003` §2 into un-audited code-mutating nodes and bypassing the 5 Merge Gates.
  2. **Silent cross-workspace privilege escalation & approval fatigue:** A lint waiver recorded once in `operator/ratified.lock` persists across all future repositories and missions without per-mission visibility, creating a permanent bypass around `[REV-SEC-02]`.
- **Failure modes of keeping `narrow-only` absolute (and how to mitigate):**
  1. **Operator inflexibility when a workflow genuinely needs a hybrid specialist:** An operator might want a doc-writing researcher or a test-running investigator.
  2. **Mitigation (already supported by architecture):** Direct the operator to either (a) invoke `@builder` directly in the console (`specs/005` §3.1), or (b) define a custom skill in `skills/custom/` (`specs/001` §3.1) attached to `builder` (which already holds worktree/sandbox permissions). This preserves `Operator Sovereignty` without corrupting canonical role boundaries.
