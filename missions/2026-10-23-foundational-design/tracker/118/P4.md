| 118 | P4 | WebP; SVG rendered safely at capture | built and merged (#747, on the operator's word of 2026-11-16; the red-team's review of the hostile-SVG fixtures had been asked for before merge): SVG capture is off by default (`[research] svg = false`, §11 decision 1; an SVG is refused `svg_off` before it is parsed) until a child-process render backstop lands in a later gate pull request; WebP is on. two new dependencies, `image-webp` 0.2.4 (MIT OR Apache-2.0, in `delphi-docs`) and `resvg` 0.50 with no default features (Apache-2.0 OR MIT; brings `usvg`, `roxmltree`, `tiny-skia` (BSD-3-Clause), `svgtypes`, `kurbo`, `simplecss` and small crates, every licence on `deny.toml`'s list; `cargo deny` not run locally). A WebP is kept as it is (`.webp`), its canvas and every frame's own header checked against the canvas and 24 million pixels before decoding, and served by the image method with `max`; the console decodes it too. An SVG is sniffed by its start, drawn at capture to a PNG of at most 1,600 pixels a side by `research_svg`, and kept beside the PNG as `<its own hash>.svg`, named in the PNG's record (`rendered_from`); an SVG never has a record of its own (`is_record_of` refuses one), so nothing serves it, and `assets check` does not list it. Bounds: 2 MiB, no DTD, 200,000 XML nodes, 64 levels, 50,000 elements and 1,000,000 path segments once `use` and markers are counted where drawn (every shape's segments, read with `svgtypes`, wherever it is drawn, and times every marker; a loop refused; on the built tree at most 3,000,000 points), every reference read as usvg reads it (`svgtypes` IRI and FuncIRI, `href` before `xlink:href`), no two elements of one id, clip-path chains at most 4 links and no marker inside a marker (both followed through `use`), at most 64 stops a gradient, no style sheet that sets either, no `pattern`, `filter` or `mask`, at most 256 layers nested 4 deep (each clip of a chain a layer) and none over two canvases, painting of at most 200 canvases (a gradient weighs 3 plus one per 8 stops) and 200,000 dashes, one drawing at a time in the process (a drawing past 10 seconds is refused but keeps the slot until it ends, and a caller behind it is refused at once), the drawing thread's stack 256 MB of address space. Three red-team passes (2026-11-16) found the clip-chain abort and 2 GB, the marker-count bypasses, the unbounded CPU after a timeout, the WebP frame size, the SVG's name, then a clip chain through `use` and gradients with many stops, then long paths drawn many times through `use` (4.7 GB) and references spelled so the audit and usvg read them differently; each is fixed with a test. No hard memory ceiling on a drawing is possible inside the daemon (an allocation failure in usvg or tiny-skia aborts the process); a real backstop needs the drawing in a child process with memory and CPU limits, which is a new process-start site (a `spawn_sites` gate change, the operator's). A record's addresses now keep their `_` (P1's `one_line` had broken Wikipedia addresses in credit lines). Every `<image>` reference is refused by our resolver; no resources folder, no text, no fonts. The licence decision is unchanged and made before any byte is fetched. Found: an SVG with text is refused (`svg_not_drawn`), because drawing it without its words would mislead and drawing text needs fonts; most diagrams on the open web have text, so the operator should decide whether to draw text with fonts the daemon ships or reads from the system. Not verified: no real SVG from a trusted host was captured; the app's bridge change (`.webp` in the library form) was not run (`app/` is outside CI) |
